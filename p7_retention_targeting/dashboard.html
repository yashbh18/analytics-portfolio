Who should we contact first?

A retention-targeting tool built on a propensity scorecard I trained in SQL on real Olist marketplace data (snapshot Sep 2017) and checked on a later period (Mar 2018, 55,524 customers). Move the sliders to see which customer groups a win-back offer pays for, and which it doesn't.

Offer assumptions

Uplift is relative: 30% means the offer turns 30% more of a group's likely buyers into buyers. Incremental profit = customers × score × uplift × AOV × margin − customers × cost. Use a hold-out group to measure real uplift before scaling.

What this means

  • Repeat buying is rare and hard to predict on this marketplace (1.2% in 180 days). I tried 3- and 5-feature scorecards; they overfit and validated worse, so the simplest recency × frequency model won.
  • The one strong signal is a previous repeat: recent customers with 2+ orders (1.1% of the base) come back 5× more often than the rest.
  • Paid discounts don't pay here. With default assumptions, the best group breaks even at about R$1.4 per contact, so only near-free channels (email, push, WhatsApp opt-ins) make money.
  • Next step: run the offer on the top two groups with a 20% hold-out to measure true uplift before scaling.

Data: public Olist Brazilian e-commerce dataset. Scorecard, validation and AUC are computed in SQL; see GitHub for the queries.
